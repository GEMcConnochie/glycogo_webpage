How sports nutrition apps compare

Four apps now do roughly the same job. Underneath they answer different questions, so the numbers they hand you will not match. Last checked 30 October 2026.

If you want your training to set your fuelling, there are four apps worth knowing about. Each one turns your sessions, body size and food into daily carbohydrate and calorie targets, fuelling for each session, and something for race day. On the surface they look interchangeable.

They are not. What separates them is where the targets come from, whether they use your own physiology rather than a population estimate, whether they show their workings, and what happens when a day does not go to plan. Below is the same set of rows filled in for all four, then the differences that actually change the numbers.

Where the numbers come from

Most apps start with an estimated resting metabolic rate, add the cost of your sessions and turn the total into macro targets. It is a sound way to manage weight, and three of the four do exactly that. It does not tell you how much energy is left for everything other than training, relative to your fat-free mass: the number that matters for hormonal function, bone health and the low energy availability problems that build up quietly over a season.

Glycogo sets daily requirements from energy availability and training expenditure, then lets you set macro ranges and meal distribution around them, and tracks availability across the day: projected levels, the largest hourly deficit, and time spent below target. Hexis shows availability live through the day with Live Energy, worked from an estimated resting metabolic rate. Fuelin and Avitu do not compute it within the day at all.

Effort is not the same as cost. Two athletes can hold the same heart rate at very different metabolic costs, and the difference shows up in what a session takes out of you. Glycogo takes cycling FTP and gross efficiency, running critical speed and running economy, and swimming CSS with propulsive efficiency, then estimates the carbohydrate and fat each session uses. Hexis asks for FTP, critical speed and cycling efficiency, so cycling and running costs come from your physiology there as well, but running economy and swimming are left out. Fuelin takes FTP and threshold pace. Avitu takes FTP alone, so cycling has a threshold to work from while running and swimming do not. If you train on a power meter or a watch with a threshold estimate you trust, this is the part that moves the numbers most. What comes out of it matters as much as what goes in: only Glycogo turns those inputs into a per-session carbohydrate and fat split.

You can see the same engine on its own in the free fat and carb utilization calculator: grams of fat and carbohydrate per hour at every intensity, shaped by your fitness and the session.

Session carbohydrate, race day and the next day

Gut tolerance is individual, trainable and different by sport: 90 g/h on the bike is not 90 g/h running. Glycogo lets you set carbohydrate-per-hour ranges by sport and GI tolerance, or start from the recommended targets. Hexis and Fuelin apply a fixed hourly target that you cannot change in the app unless you are on a coached plan, and Avitu applies one you cannot change at all. That is fine until you are gut training towards something higher. The free carb fuel-ratio calculator shows how a product or a mix of foods splits against the glucose to fructose ratios used in the research.

Race day is where a fuelling app either helps or gets in the way. Four hours on a rolling course is not the same as four hours on a climb, and where the aid stations fall decides what you carry. Glycogo plans by course profile and section, including the products you will use and the stations you will pass, with an 8 to 12 g/kg carb load you can switch on for the days before. Fuelin gives race week and race day guidance without course planning. Avitu sets carb loading and in-activity targets around a key event. Hexis has no in-app race planner.

The quieter difference is the next day. Glycogo lets you mark meals as completed and carries any shortfall or surplus into tomorrow's meal targets, so a light day on the bike is accounted for rather than quietly forgotten. Hexis and Fuelin allow meals to be completed but do not carry the difference forward. Avitu offers neither.

What it costs

Glycogo's core features are free; Premium adds AI chat, voice, photo and barcode logging for about €3.50 or $4 a month. Hexis is €16.99 a month or €109.99 a year with no free tier. Fuelin is $29 a month or $139 a year with no general free trial, and is the most expensive of the four. Avitu is €4.99 a month or €49.99 a year with a 14 day trial that does not ask for a card. Prices are as displayed on 30 October 2026 and are set by each vendor, so check before you subscribe.

What this does not compare

Coaching support, dietitian review, food database size, integrations beyond Intervals.icu and how pleasant each app is to use day to day. All of it matters, and none of it is comparable from public documentation. Nor does it rank the four: the right one depends on whether you want a weight management tool with fuelling attached, or a fuelling tool built from your own physiology. The table is there so you can check the specific thing you care about.
