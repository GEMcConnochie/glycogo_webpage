The physiology behind the curves

Measurement basis. Substrate use during exercise is quantified by indirect calorimetry: because fat and carbohydrate oxidation yield different volumes of CO₂ per litre of O₂ consumed, the non-protein respiratory exchange ratio (RER = VCO₂ / VO₂) identifies the fuel mix. RER 0.70 corresponds to near-exclusive fat oxidation, RER 1.00 to near-exclusive carbohydrate, and RER ≈ 0.85 to an even energy split (Peronnet & Massicotte, 1991). Each point on the chart is reported as an implied RER with matching carbohydrate and fat oxidation rates in grams per hour, converted with non-protein energy equivalents (carbohydrate ≈ 4 kcal/g, fat ≈ 9 kcal/g).

Intensity: the crossover and fatmax. As relative intensity rises, catecholamine drive, type-II motor-unit recruitment, and the energy rate demanded per unit of muscle shift supply toward muscle glycogen and blood glucose, and the relative fat contribution falls: the classic “crossover” (Brooks & Mercier, 1994; Romijn et al., 1993). In trained athletes, absolute fat oxidation commonly peaks around 45–65 % VO₂max (the “fatmax” zone), although with substantial individual variation (Achten & Jeukendrup, 2003).

Availability: what moves the curve. Substrate availability influences oxidation. Pre-exercise and during-exercise carbohydrate raise carbohydrate oxidation at a given intensity; habitual carbohydrate intake and training status reshape glycogen stores and oxidative capacity; low-carbohydrate availability raises the relative fat contribution (Romijn et al., 1993; Burke et al., 2011). The model further adjusts for session duration, sex, age, and body mass, reflecting the group-level effects quantified in modelling analyses of submaximal exercise (Rothschild et al., 2022).

The interactive readout also reports the model’s implied RER at each point. Because the model maps fuel split to RER through a smooth physiological response curve rather than the strictly linear non-protein approximation, its implied RER can sit a few thousandths above the linear table at high intensities; the static table therefore lists only the internally consistent energy split and oxidation rates.

Limitations. Outputs are group-level predictions of substrate partitioning for the inputs you supply, not measurements of your metabolism. Day-to-day variability, training status, diet history, heat, and gut tolerance all move real-world values. Use the explorer to compare scenarios and structure fueling, not as metabolic testing.

Frequently asked questions

What is RER and what does RER 0.85 mean?

RER (respiratory exchange ratio) is the volume of CO₂ exhaled divided by O₂ consumed, the core measurement of indirect calorimetry. RER 0.70 ≈ 100 % of energy from fat, RER 1.00 ≈ 100 % from carbohydrate, and RER ≈ 0.85 marks an even 50/50 energy split. The chart’s RER readout is the model’s implied RER at that point.

At what intensity does fuel use become 50/50?

It is athlete-specific. For the default reference athlete here (70 kg, male, 34 years, moderate-carbohydrate diet) the crossover sits near RPE 20 on the CR-100 scale (easy-moderate). Higher carbohydrate availability shifts it toward lower intensities; low-carbohydrate availability or fat-adapted training shifts it higher.

Why does carbohydrate intake shift the curve toward carbohydrate?

Substrate availability influences oxidation. Pre-exercise and during-exercise carbohydrate raise plasma glucose delivery and reduce the relative reliance on fat at the same absolute intensity; habitual low-carbohydrate intake does the opposite. These are the acute and chronic effects the diet sliders reproduce.

Are the g/h numbers intake targets?

No. They estimate endogenous oxidation, what your body burns from muscle glycogen, blood glucose, and plasma fatty acids. Exogenous fueling targets (for example, 60–90 g/h of multiple-transportable carbohydrate during long sessions) are a separate prescription that only partially overlaps oxidation.

How accurate are the estimates?

They apply group-level relationships from indirect-calorimetry research to the inputs you choose, so individual values vary with fitness, training status, diet history, and environment. Use the tool to compare scenarios and plan fueling structure; for individualised session fueling, the Glycogo app incorporates measured power, heart rate, and energy expenditure.
