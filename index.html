FAQ

Common Questions

How Glycogo calculates training fuel, daily targets, and within-day energy balance without the jargon.

Workouts & device calories
What does “Use device session calories” do?
  • When enabled: Glycogo starts from the calorie value reported by your synced device or platform (Garmin, Strava, Wahoo, Intervals.icu, and similar), then converts it to net active calories for fuelling targets.
  • When disabled: Glycogo estimates session burn using your sport settings (for example cycling power, running pace, swim pace, efficiency preferences, and body weight) instead of the device number.

Even with device calories enabled, Glycogo will still show a lower figure than your watch. The app removes the resting portion of the device estimate so daily nutrition stays consistent.

Why do workout calories in Glycogo differ from my Garmin, Wahoo, or Strava?

During exercise your body draws energy from resting metabolism and from the work your muscles perform. Sports watches usually report the combined total. Glycogo separates the active portion so it is not counted twice, because your daily meal targets already include your baseline resting needs.

How does Glycogo sync training sessions?

Glycogo connects to Intervals.icu, which can pull planned and completed workouts from major fitness platforms. Imported sessions drive pre-session, during-session, and recovery fuel targets for that day.

You can also add or edit sessions manually inside the app.

How should I rate my session RPE?

When you add or edit a session manually, rate the overall difficulty of the whole workout on the Borg CR100 scale (1–100). This is session RPE: how hard the entire session felt relative to what you could maximally sustain for ~1hr, not the peak effort of your hardest interval.

A long aerobic ride with a few surges usually rates lower than a steady threshold block, even if peak power or pace was high.

As you move the slider in the app, a short label updates (for example “Moderate”, “Strong”, or “Very Hard”) to match the CR100 bands:

  • Very Easy to Easy (roughly 1–20): Recovery or relaxed aerobic work.
  • Steady Easy to Moderate (roughly 20–40): Comfortable endurance with some focus.
  • Moderately Hard to Strong (roughly 40–60): Purposeful training with clear effort.
  • Hard to Very Hard (roughly 60–80): Demanding, sustained work.
  • Extremely Hard to Maximal (roughly 80–100): Very heavy or race-like sessions.
Tip: If you log intervals, warm up and cool-down as one session, the easy sections and recoveries between reps should lower the overall rating compared with your hardest rep.
How is RPE calculated for a synced session?

When a workout is synced, Glycogo estimates training load (shown as RPE 1–100) from the activity data. It uses the best available metric first rather than requiring you to manually enter effort.

Completed workouts are estimated in this order:

  1. Cycling power compared with your FTP (from Glycogo sport settings or Intervals.icu).
  2. Manual RPE you logged in Intervals.icu, if present.
  3. Average heart rate compared with your threshold or max heart rate.
  4. Other intensity fields from the activity when the above are missing.

Planned workouts use:

  1. Planned cycling power vs FTP.
  2. Planned pace or speed vs your critical run pace or swim CSS from sport settings.
  3. Manual RPE from Intervals.icu, then other intensity fields.

If nothing usable is available, Glycogo uses a moderate default (50) until you change it. You can always edit RPE after sync if the estimate does not match how the session felt.

Power, pace, and heart-rate estimates work best when FTP, critical pace, swim CSS, and heart-rate thresholds are set in your sport settings or on Intervals.icu.
Within-day energy balance
What is within-day energy availability?
Within-day energy availability tracks your hour-by-hour balance between what you eat and what you burn as the day unfolds.

Many apps only look at your total at the end of the day. Glycogo focuses on when you are in deficit, because prolonged low-energy periods during the day are linked to hormonal disruption and poorer recovery and body-composition outcomes, even if a large evening meal brings the daily total back to balance.

What does the within-day energy availability timeline chart show?

The timeline is your within-day energy picture, showing both projected and logged meals, training sessions, and how your cumulative balance changes through the day.

Meal and session markers are interactive: hover or tap for details. The chart highlights when you spend extended time in a notable energy deficit, and helps you adjust meal timing or size to optimize energy levels over the day.

Why does the app show a deficit when I first wake up?

Your body keeps burning energy overnight for temperature regulation, brain function, and repair. Glycogo uses a circadian model that spreads resting metabolism unevenly across the day (less overnight, more during active hours) rather than dividing it equally across 24 hours.

The negative balance you see in the morning reflects overnight energy use and gives a realistic starting point for within-day fuelling. It is expected and not a sign that something is wrong with your settings.

Fueling settings
What should my carbs-per-hour range be?

Your lower and upper bounds should reflect gut tolerance, training experience, and session demands:

  • Lower bound: Easy aerobic, recovery, or low-intensity, shorter-duration work. A common starting range is roughly 30–50 g/hr to support stable energy without overloading the gut.
  • Upper bound: Hard intervals, threshold work, or race-specific sessions where glycogen use is high.
  • General athletes: Up to about 90 g/hr is a widely used upper guideline for sustained hard exercise (ACSM guidelines).
  • Higher-output, gut-trained athletes: Some athletes tolerate and use more than 90 g/hr, especially when total energy demand is very high or when staying fuelled across long or multi-session days. Higher intakes require deliberate gut training in practice. You can raise your upper limit as needed or as you adapt.
What should my minimum session duration be?

This setting controls when Glycogo adds during-session carbohydrate targets. Lowering the threshold adds during-session fuel to shorter workouts. Raising it keeps fuelling focused on truly long efforts.

The default is 60 minutes, which suits most endurance training, with 60–90 minutes a good range. Shorter sessions often do not need intra-workout carbs.

What is the high-carb fuelling setting for a session?

When you enable High-carb fuelling on an individual workout, Glycogo always includes during-session carbohydrate targets for that session at the upper end of your carbs/hr range, even if it is shorter than your minimum duration setting or at a lower intensity.

This is useful for gut training (practising higher carb intakes in training) or when pre-session fuel is limited and you still need on-the-go carbohydrates during the workout.

What is the difference between during-session carbs vs total carbs added for the session?

During-session carbs are the carbohydrates you aim to take in while exercising, based on your carbs-per-hour settings and how long the workout lasts.

Total carbs added for the session is the full fuelling bump that workout contributes to your day: during-session carbs plus the pre-session and recovery carbohydrate allocated around that workout.

Daily carbohydrate targets then combine your baseline meals with the total added from all sessions on that day.

What is the “Carb Load” setting used for?

Carb loading increases your baseline daily carbohydrate target for a chosen day to support fuller muscle glycogen stores before a key event or long effort.

Tip: Training on a carb-load day still adds session fuel on top of the elevated baseline. If you also have a workout that day, consider the lower end of your carb-load range so the total goal stays practical.
Meal timing
Why does the app schedule a pre-session snack?

Pre-session snacks help top up carbohydrate stores, which is particularly important for pre-breakfast training or before hard or long work. By default the app places this about an hour before your session starts. You can change the timing or turn pre-session snacks off in settings.

Why did my default meal times shift after I added a workout?

If your meal’s default time in settings (for example Breakfast at 07:30, Lunch at 12:30, Dinner at 18:30) falls within a workout session block, Glycogo automatically shifts it to avoid overlap and ensure adequate digestion time.

The meal is moved to either 60 minutes before the session starts or directly to the end of the session, selecting whichever option is closest to the meal’s original default time.

With multiple sessions on one day, fuelling is planned per workout and daily targets reflect the combined training load. See multiple workouts on the same day.

How does Glycogo handle multiple workouts on the same day?

Each session gets its own pre-session, during-session, and recovery nutrition timing. Net training energy and carbohydrate needs for the day reflect the combined load from all sessions.

Sport settings & calorie estimates
What is the fueling heat map on the settings screen?

It shows how your planned carbohydrate intake per hour varies with session duration and RPE. Use it as a visual guide to how fuelling ramps up as efforts get longer or harder.

Use it alongside your carbs-per-hour bounds to check that easy and hard sessions produce targets that match your training and gut tolerance.

What if I do not know my FTP, critical pace, or swim CSS?

Leave those fields blank or at zero and Glycogo falls back to standard sport-specific estimates scaled to your body weight. You still get a usable calorie and fuelling range; entering personal benchmarks later will refine it.

What if I do not know my efficiency for cycling, running, or swimming?

Leave the efficiency sliders at their defaults. They represent typical values for each sport. You do not need lab testing to start using Glycogo.

  • Cycling: Default reflects a typical gross efficiency for trained riders. Move left if you are newer to cycling or a more fast-twitch dominant athlete, and vice versa.
  • Running: Default reflects a typical economy. Less economical runners need more fuel at the same pace; well-trained, economical runners need less.
  • Swimming: Default reflects typical propulsive efficiency in the water. Less efficient technique or higher drag means higher calorie needs at the same pace.

You can adjust these settings over time based on feel, recovery, and performance.

How does cycling efficiency affect calorie burn?

Cycling efficiency describes how much of your total energy output reaches the pedals as mechanical power:

  • Lower efficiency: More energy is lost as heat for the same power, so calorie burn is higher.
  • Higher efficiency: Less total energy is needed to hold the same power, so calorie burn is lower.

Adjust the slider in settings if your real-world fuelling feedback suggests your defaults are too high or too low.

How does running economy affect calorie burn?

Running economy works on the same principle as cycling efficiency. It reflects how much energy you spend to sustain a given pace. Less economical runners burn more calories at the same speed; more economical runners burn less.

Why is swimming calorie estimation sensitive to technique and pace?

Water creates far more resistance than air. Small increases in speed can demand a disproportionately large rise in effort, and technique has a big impact on how much energy is spent fighting drag and staying aligned in the water, so calorie burn should not be based on speed alone.

Glycogo’s swim settings let you account for your personal efficiency. If you are unsure, start conservative and adjust based on how sessions feel and recover.

Using the app
What does “Suggest quantities” do?

When you log or plan foods, Suggest quantities recommends portion sizes to help close your remaining macro gaps for that meal, especially carbohydrates and calories, without manual trial and error.

What are meal templates and the meal builder?

Templates let you save combinations of foods you eat often and reload them in one step. The meal builder stages foods before you log them to a meal, so you can search, scan, or add items, edit amounts, and review the full meal before logging.

What happens when I mark a meal complete?

Marking a meal complete tells Glycogo you have eaten what you logged for that meal. That meal’s targets lock to your actual intake, and any remaining calories and macros for the day are redistributed across your still-open meals using your meal distribution settings.

Your within-day timeline and projections update too, so the rest of the day reflects logged intake.

Are my changes saved if sync is temporarily unavailable?

Yes. Edits are stored on your device first and sync to your account when connectivity returns. Retry runs in the background so you do not need to re-enter meals because of a brief connection issue.
